Europe has spent the last few years turning cybersecurity into law. NIS2 for
critical sectors, DORA for finance, the Cyber Resilience Act for anything shipped
with a digital element, eIDAS 2 for identity and trust services, the AI Act layered
over all of it, plus the sectoral rules that rarely make the headlines, like the
network code for electricity, Part-IS for aviation, and the 5G Toolbox. Each one
arrived with its own regulators, certification schemes, deadlines and, eventually,
its own body of implementation material.

That material is real and a lot of it is very good. It is also scattered: across
twenty-seven national agencies, several publishing only in their own language;
across ENISA, the ESAs, ETSI and CENELEC; across EU-funded research projects that
shipped working code and then went quiet; and across GitHub organisations that
almost nothing links to from anywhere obvious, among them CIRCL, CERT Polska,
ANSSI, BSI and the EU Digital Identity Wallet. Meanwhile the tooling most teams
reach for by default is American, and the European equivalent, often the one the
regulator itself built and published, goes unused.

So this is the index. Every entry has had its URL opened and its content checked
against the claim made about it. Anything that could not be verified was dropped
rather than guessed, which is why some sections here are short: that is the honest
shape of the ecosystem, not a gap in the research.

**Arrived here with a specific obligation rather than a topic?**
[`docs/BY-REGULATION.md`](docs/BY-REGULATION.md) slices the same entries by the
instrument that puts them in scope: NIS2, DORA, the CRA, GDPR, the AI Act,
eIDAS, the certification schemes, the EECC and the post-quantum transition.

### What belongs here

A resource earns a place if it addresses an EU regulation (NIS2, DORA, GDPR, CRA,
the AI Act, eIDAS), an EU or national technical standard or certification scheme
(EUCC, EUCS, SecNumCloud, BSI C5, IT-Grundschutz, ENS), or is maintained by a
European body, government, university or EU-funded project.

Standards and agency guidance count for as much as code here. A PDF from ANSSI is
a first-class entry, because for a lot of these problems the PDF *is* the
deliverable.
Where there is tooling, free and open-source comes first; closed products appear
only where they are sovereign infrastructure or an official registry with no open
equivalent, and they are marked `[Commercial]` so you can see them coming.

What stays out: generic security tooling with no EU angle however good it is,
vendor marketing, exam prep, conference listings, and raw legal text with no
implementation value beyond the regulation itself.

## Legend

| Marker | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `[Open Source]` | Source code is public under an open-source licence. |
| `[EU-Hosted]` | Service operated on infrastructure run by an EU body or member state. |
| `[Commercial]` | Closed or paid. Present only as sovereign infrastructure or an official registry, always with a note saying why. |
| `Tool` `Standard` `Framework` `Guideline` `Certification Scheme` `Report` `Feed` | What kind of resource it is. |
| 🇪🇺 🇩🇪 🇫🇷 … | Who maintains it. |

A ⚠️ means `confidence: medium`: the resource is real and the link resolves, but
its EU framing or its maintenance status rests on thinner evidence than the rest.
Worth a second look before you rely on it.

## Contributing

Corrections are as welcome as additions, arguably more so. A link that has rotted,
a project quietly archived, an entry whose EU framing does not survive a careful
read: all of those are worth an issue.

Open a [resource proposal](../../issues/new?template=add_resource.yml) or send a
pull request editing the relevant file under [`data/resources/`](data/resources).
`README.md` is generated from that data, so please do not edit it directly; CI
will tell you off. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) has the scope rules and the
one bar that really matters: **do not add a resource you have not opened yourself.**

If you work at a national agency or CSIRT and we have listed your work wrongly, or
missed something you think belongs, please say so. That feedback is worth more than
any amount of searching from the outside.
